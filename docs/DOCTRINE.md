# Scienter research model — draft 0.1.0

This taxonomy organizes comparison; it is not an additive legal test, legal opinion, or classifier of whether scienter exists. Factor weights are retrieval choices only. It needs a securities litigator's review before production use. The research below is historical, limited to selected primary judicial opinions read through public mirrors, and has not been Shepardized or KeyCited. There is no claim that later decisions, amendments or all exceptions have been exhausted.

## Governing framework

The PSLRA requires particularized facts supporting a strong inference of the requisite state of mind. [Tellabs, Inc. v. Makor Issues & Rights, Ltd., 551 U.S. 308, 323–26 (2007)](https://www.govinfo.gov/app/details/USREPORTS-551/USREPORTS-551-308) requires collective consideration and comparison with plausible innocent explanations. Its pleading inquiry is not trial proof, a probability output, or a count of favorable factors. Tellabs is Supreme Court doctrine, not a Second/Ninth case-level comparator. It vacated and remanded rather than deciding that the complaint ultimately met its newly articulated standard.

In the Second Circuit, [ATSI Communications, Inc. v. Shaar Fund, Ltd., 493 F.3d 87, 99, 104 (2007)](https://openjurist.org/493/f3d/87) describes motive and opportunity or strong circumstantial evidence of conscious misbehavior or recklessness, alongside Tellabs. A generic opportunity to profit does not automatically suffice. ATSI concerns an issuer's financing/manipulation claims, which materially limits comparison to ordinary investor issuer-disclosure class actions.

In the Ninth Circuit, [Zucco Partners, LLC v. Digimarc Corp., 552 F.3d 981, 991–95 (2009)](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/ca9/06-35758/0635758-2011-02-25.html) discusses intentional conduct or deliberate recklessness, individual allegations and holistic evaluation. Its CW analysis asks both whether the source is described with enough specificity for reliability and personal knowledge, and whether the reported statements indicate scienter. Recording title, duties, tenure, reporting chain, firsthand basis, event dates and corroboration is more useful than treating the number of CWs as a legal score.

[Novak v. Kasaks, 216 F.3d 300, 311–15 (2d Cir. 2000)](https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/us-2nd-circuit/1436176.html) is a pre-Tellabs inventory/accounting and confidential-source example. It found scienter sufficiently alleged, but remanded for reconsideration of particularity rather than declaring every pleading requirement satisfied. The seed's “survived” label concerns scienter only.

[In re Daou Systems, Inc. Securities Litigation, 411 F.3d 1006, 1022–23 (9th Cir. 2005)](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/F3/411/1006/497079/) is also pre-Tellabs. Detailed alleged accounting involvement supported an inference for some defendants, while the court affirmed dismissal of Moragne. The seed therefore preserves a mixed result rather than counting it as an unqualified plaintiff win. The linked amended opinion is dated June 21, 2005.

Corporate attribution cannot be collapsed into whether a named individual remains a defendant. [Teamsters Local 445 Freight Division Pension Fund v. Dynex Capital Inc., 531 F.3d 190, 195–97 (2d Cir. 2008)](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/ca2/06-2902/06-2902-cv_opn-2011-03-27.html) allowed the possibility of adequate corporate pleading without adequate pleading against a specifically named individual, but found the allegations there deficient. It was an interlocutory appeal from a partial denial, with dismissal and an opportunity to replead directed on remand.

[In re NVIDIA Corp. Securities Litigation, 768 F.3d 1046 (9th Cir. 2014)](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/ca9/11-17708/11-17708-2014-10-02.html) supplies a historical product-defect/core-operations comparison. It is not the later NVIDIA cryptocurrency litigation. The seed is limited to its scienter analysis; it does not represent its discussion of Item 303 as the current law of omissions.

## Coding rules

- `present` means the factor is discussed or alleged, not that it favors the plaintiff, is true, or is sufficient. Characterization supplies the court's treatment. `absent` requires an explicit source; silence is `unknown`.
- `specific`, `general`, and `mixed` describe the extracted material's detail; they are not judicial ratings or probabilities.
- Every known factor requires a quotation, pinpoint and source. Quote containment only prevents unsupported strings; it does not establish correct attribution, source integrity, completeness or sound legal interpretation.
- Defendant, statement and period should be resolved separately before promotion to a production corpus. Current seed records are opinion-level summaries and cannot capture every claim or defendant disposition.
- Record investigations separately from charges, findings, admissions and no-admission settlements. A parallel SEC or DOJ matter has no fixed weight or proven causal effect here.
- Magnitude requires denominators, duration requires dates, and stock sales require holdings and a baseline. Hindsight, job title, departures, GAAP errors and generic incentives are not standalone conclusions.
- Review opposing inferences and the court's combined reasoning. No weighted factor total may be described as the legal strong-inference test.

## Required corpus review

A production study needs inclusion/exclusion rules, duplicate/related-case controls, complaint-version and defendant-level outcomes, published/unpublished coverage, appeal linkage, legal-update review, disagreement adjudication between reviewers, and an inter-rater quality assessment. District motions, final appeals and interlocutory appeals must remain separable. Summary judgment and trial are outside this draft schema. No complete production empirical dataset has been supplied.
